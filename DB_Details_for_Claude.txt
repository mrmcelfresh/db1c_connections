We are starting with 80 million rows of DOT DB1C airline ticket data, sourced from the BTS website for six months of travel between July 2026 and December 2026.  This produces ~80 million rows or a 40% ticket sample of domestic O&D traffic from reporting carriers.

Our project will be to build data visualizations and conduct analysis to determine if and how dwell times for connecting itineraries correlate to customer preference.  One view of this will be to look at passenger share by dwell time.  We will also want to be able to do direct hub to hub comparisons (e.g. are dwell times different for connectionsnce o via CLT compared to ATL or IAD).  We will also want to be able to compare results across carriers.  We will want to know if the conclusions of our analysis change when WN or other low cost carriers are excluded and we only compare legacy carriers against one another.

Since our focus is on connecting itineraries, all ticket data that contains only non-stop flights (as defined by trip breaks) can be excluded.  This does not need to be written to the database.  Complex itineraries with double, triple, or more connects within trip breaks can also be excluded as outliers.  Only tickets that included at least 1 one-stop itinerary over one of the hubs defined in Connecting_Airport_List need to be written to the database.

While we are not excluding any data based on circuity, but will need to be able to use this information for filtering/querying downstream.  We are using hub dwell time (connecting time) as a proxy for total itinerary travel time.  If the circuity of two different routings for a common O&D vary significantly, dwell time becomes a poor predictor of this.  We want to retain all specified routings in the data, but want to plan for this grouping/filtering by like hubs with the option to exclude routings that are likely to have highly dissimilar sum of flight time.

Based on the filtering above, we likely can discard many of the right most columns in the Parquet files, which are retained for complex and outlying itineraries.

DB1C shows only total ticket revenue (TotalAmt).  Since not every ticket includes the same number of trip breaks, this will not be an apples to apples comparison.  We will need to determine a way to allocate revenue across trip breaks.  Dividing revenue evenly across trip break portions of the itinerary is likely the easiest path.  This will be inaccurate since underlying airline technology prices each trip break portion independently, but it’s not clear that the data is sufficiently robust to offer a better approach to estimating the revenue allocation to each trip break.  AI coding agents reading this markdown file are welcome to make recommendations on the best way to handle this.
